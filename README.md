inspired in part by the [norsk experiment](https://www.apronus.com/norsk/) and [monolingual fieldwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolingual_fieldwork), i am attempting to decipher the thai language purely by watching tv shows.

the current rules:
- no subtitles
- no dictionaries
- look up _nothing about the language whatsoever_
- try to avoid picking up the script as much as possible (even by accident)
- it's okay to rewind but not to slow down or otherwise manipulate the audio

## some questions you might have
### why are you doing this?
- it makes the language learning process feel like solving a puzzle and i love puzzles
- i was wondering if it was possible to do
- it's cool if i succeed
- it's a silly story to tell

### isn't that extremely inefficient?
oh absolutely, no one in their right mind should try to learn a language like this.

### why thai?
i was looking for a language which:
- is completely unrelated to any language i've learned anything about (and ideally not too influenced by any of them)
    - (if you know me you will now that this narrows things down a *lot*)
- offers a lifetime supply of content that would keep me engaged
    - if i'm going to be spend 1000+ hours understanding basically nothing that is said, the visuals had better be entertaining...
    - ... and guess what appears to be the second best language for gay romance shows after english (per 豆瓣 ratings)? :D
